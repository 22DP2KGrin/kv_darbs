Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Introduction

In this lesson, you will learn about the difference between Present Perfect and Past Simple tenses. These tenses are often confused by English learners because they both refer to actions in the past, but they are used in different contexts.

Present Perfect vs Past Simple: Key Differences

Present Perfect (have/has + past participle)

Used for:

  • Actions that started in the past and continue to the present
  • Recent actions (when the time is not mentioned)
  • Life experiences (without specific time)
  • Actions with results in the present

Examples:

  • I have lived in London for 5 years. (I still live there)
  • She has just finished her homework.
  • Have you ever visited Paris?
  • I have lost my keys. (Result: I don't have them now)

Past Simple (regular: verb + ed, or irregular forms)

Used for:

  • Completed actions in the past
  • Actions at a specific time in the past
  • Series of completed actions in the past
  • Habits or states in the past

Examples:

  • I lived in London for 5 years. (I don't live there now)
  • She finished her homework at 6 PM.
  • Did you visit Paris last summer?
  • I lost my keys yesterday.
